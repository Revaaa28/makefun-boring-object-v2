# Candidates — The Most Boring Object Reviewed in One Line (v2)

Standing line (R1 rank 1708/1896, 36.30%):
"My coaster's only job is to sit under a drink and do nothing. It has been employee of the month for six years."

Judge brief, in order:
1. One-line review of a boring everyday object, funny and true
2. Laugh at a real, recognisable detail of the object, not a joke that fits anything
3. True: observation matches how the object actually behaves; laugh arrives with recognition
4. One line, <=200 chars, every word earning its place
5. Lands fast: turn comes early, last words sharpen it

## Fresh candidates

1. Silica gel packet: "My silica gel packet has spent three years silently absorbing moisture in a shoebox, yet its only label is DO NOT EAT — wildly optimistic about how tempting that job looks."
2. Paperclip: "This paperclip has faithfully held together a 40-page report nobody has opened since 2019; we are both just pretending the separation would matter."
3. Rubber band: "The rubber band in my junk drawer has held nothing together for four years, yet the one time I stretched it, it snapped at me like I was the one who had given up."
4. Doorstop: "My doorstop has held the same door open for nine years, and its reward for perfect attendance is getting kicked into a corner every time someone wants it shut."
5. Pizza box table: "The tiny plastic table in the pizza box exists only to stop cheese touching cardboard, a bodyguard who is thrown away with the thing he protected."
6. Allen key: "My spare Allen key fits no furniture I still own, yet I keep it in the drawer like a pension for a job it did once, badly, in 2021."
7. Tea towel: "This tea towel has dried ten thousand dishes and still hangs there damp, the only employee who gets wetter the harder it works."
8. Grout: "The grout between my bathroom tiles has spent a decade doing nothing but get dirty in a perfectly straight line, and somehow that is the job done correctly."
9. Blank notebook page: "The first page of this notebook has been too precious to write on for two years, which is an impressive career for a sheet of paper whose job is being written on."
10. Bread clip: "This plastic bread clip has kept a bag closed for three weeks past the bread going mouldy, loyally guarding something nobody should ever eat again."

## Manual ranking (brief + pairwise)

- #1 silica gel wins on criterion 2 outright: DO NOT EAT is a detail unique to this
  object — it cannot be transplanted to any other object. True behaviour (silently
  absorbs moisture in a shoebox) gives recognition on criterion 3, and the final
  clause "wildly optimistic about how tempting that job looks" sharpens instead
  of trailing off (criterion 5).
- #2 paperclip is strong and true, but the "pretending separation would matter"
  turn is softer and more sentimental than funny.
- #3 rubber band: the snap is recognisable, but the personification at the end
  ("like I was the one who had given up") is a generic joke shape.
- #5 pizza table is funny but the object is less everyday/boring-universal than
  silica gel, and "bodyguard" is a familiar gag.
- #4, #6, #7, #8, #9, #10 all rely on employee/pension/loyalty personification —
  the same generic family as the standing coaster line's "employee of the month".
  That family is exactly what graded 36.30%: it would fit anything.

## Pairwise vs standing

Standing coaster: generic personification, no coaster-specific detail (no ring,
no condensation, no cork) — fails criterion 2 against almost any specific line.
Winner #1 vs standing: #1 has a unique recognisable detail, a true behaviour,
and a sharper final twist. CLEARLY stronger on criteria 2, 3 and 5, equal on
1 and 4. Not a tie.
